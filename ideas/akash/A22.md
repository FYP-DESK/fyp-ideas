## 3. On your specific question: "noir kyc => zk kyc is the smallest?"

**No.** ZK-KYC (Idea 30 above) is one of the *hardest* technically. It needs:

- Deep cryptography (ZK proofs, circuits, provers, verifiers)
- NADRA integration or a credible mock that stands in for it
- Understanding of identity law, AML/CFT, PVARA
- Production-grade proof verification

That is not a "small" project. It is a startup. The *concept* is elegant, but the execution is heavy.

**What IS small and closest to your interest:** a **ZK identity proof prototype** — a scoped demonstration that:

- Takes a mock CNIC verification (or a real NADRA test credential if you can get one)
- Generates a ZK proof (using Noir/Nargo or Circom) that proves "this person is a valid Pakistani national, over 18, residing in X" **without revealing the CNIC number**
- A verifier (another student project, a mock fintech) checks the proof and accepts/rejects

That prototype — a proof-of-concept, not a production identity layer — is a strong FYP. It shows you understand ZK, you can use Noir, you understand the identity problem, and you can build a working demo. Scope it to a semester or two. That is the right slice.

## 4. The two best FYP paths for you

### Path A — If you want to flex technical depth (cryptography, ZK, cutting-edge)

**Project:** ZK Identity Proof Prototype (scoped-down ZK-KYC)

- **What you build:** A Noir/Nargo circuit that proves a credential property without revealing the underlying data. A prover CLI that generates a proof from a mock NADRA-style credential. A verifier that checks it. A simple web UI that demonstrates the flow end-to-end.
- **Why it works as FYP:** Cutting-edge, defensible, demonstrable, scoped. You are not building Pakistan's identity layer — you are proving you can build a ZK identity proof. That is a complete, honest FYP.
- **Skill signal:** ZK circuits, Noir/Nargo, prover/verifier architecture, identity/privacy concepts, systems integration.
- **What it is NOT:** A production KYC platform. You are not pitching yourself as the grantor of Pakistan's identity infrastructure.

### Path B — If you want to ship a real, usable product (full-stack, practical)

**Project:** Freelancer Escrow Platform (A21-8) — scoped to PK freelancers

- **What you build:** Client deposits USDT/USDC (or PKR via Raast integration if you can get API access) → smart contract holds → released on milestone approval. Dispute flagging. Basic dashboard for both sides. NADRA identity verification (Verisys API or mock) for both parties.
- **Why it works as FYP:** Real users exist (1.5M+ Pakistani freelancers). You can pilot with a small community. Full-stack skills + smart contracts + payments + identity. Revenue path is clear (fee per transaction).
- **Skill signal:** Full-stack development, smart contract security, payment integration, user-facing product, basic compliance.
- **What it is NOT:** A full freelance platform competing with Upwork. You are building an escrow layer — narrow, clear, shippable.

### Path C — If you want the calmest, most honest, lowest-drama option

**Project:** Raast Payment Integration SDK + Demo App (Idea #3 in Group 1)

- **What you build:** A clean SDK that wraps Raast's instant payment API. A demo app (freelancer payment collector, or small merchant payment page) that shows it working. Documentation that a third developer could use.
- **Why it works as FYP:** You are learning Pakistan's dominant payment rail. You are building something reusable. You are not chasing blockchain hype — you are building on the actual infrastructure Pakistan is adopting. That is calm, real, and valuable.
- **Skill signal:** Payment systems, API design, SDK development, real PK financial infrastructure.
- **What it is NOT:** A startup pitch. It is a student project that demonstrates you understand how money moves in Pakistan today.

## 5. How to decide

Ask yourself one question: **In 5 years, do you want to be the person who built a ZK identity proof prototype, or the person who built a working freelancer escrow that real Pakistanis used, or the person who built the SDK everyone uses to integrate Raast?**

- Path A → you become the cryptography/ZK person. Harder, less immediate use, higher technical ceiling.
- Path B → you become the product/payments person. Real users, real feedback, clearer business path.
- Path C → you become the infrastructure person. Less glamorous, deeply useful, employer-valuable.

All three are honest. None requires you to perform "extra ordinary." All three are FYP-worthy. Pick the one that matches the person you are actually building toward — not the persona you are escaping.

## 6. What not to do

- Do not announce yourself as "the stablecoin person for Pakistan" before you have built anything. That is chasing a persona, not building.
- Do not pick Idea 1 (remittance rail) or Idea 2 (ZK-KYC full platform) as your FYP — they are startup-scale, not student-scale, unless you scope them to a tiny demonstrable slice.
- Do not over-explain your motivation in the FYP documentation. Show the work. Let it speak.
